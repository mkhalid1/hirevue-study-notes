# Pattern and logic games

**In one line:** puzzles where you find the rule behind a set of shapes, letters or numbers, or work through a set of conditions to reach the only answer that fits.

HireVue does not publish its game names or rules. Its articles describe cognitive games where candidates **detect patterns in different images** and solve problems within rules, and they place these under the **Working with Information** domain: problem-solving, critical thinking and decision-making. HireVue also says its games adapt, moving you up or down levels as you go. This guide trains the reasoning, using text versions of the kinds of puzzle these games are built on.

## What it measures

- **Pattern recognition:** seeing what changes from one item to the next, and by how much.
- **Rule induction:** working out a hidden rule from a few examples, then applying it.
- **Deduction:** taking given conditions as true and finding what must follow.
- **Planning:** putting steps in an order that satisfies every condition.

These are close to what psychologists call fluid reasoning: solving new problems you have not been taught a method for.

## How the questions look

In our own words, typical shapes are:

- a row or grid of shapes with one missing, and options to choose from
- a sequence of letters or symbols that change in a regular way
- a set of rules ("the blue tile must be next to the red tile") and a board to arrange
- a short logic puzzle with exactly one answer

On screen these are usually visual. The underlying rules are the same ones you can practise in text.

## A method that works

1. **Name what changes.** Look at two neighbouring items and list every difference: position, number, size, colour, direction, shading.
2. **Check how it changes.** Is it the same step each time (add one, rotate 90 degrees), or does the step itself grow?
3. **Test the rule on every item, not just the first two.** A rule that fits two items often breaks on the third.
4. **For grids, check rows and columns.** Sometimes the rule runs across, sometimes down, sometimes both.
5. **For logic, write the conditions as short notes.** "B before A. C after A. D first." Then place the fixed items first.
6. **For "must be true" questions, look for a case that breaks it.** If you can imagine one situation that fits all the facts but makes the statement false, the statement does not have to be true.
7. **Eliminate.** When options are offered, ruling out the clearly wrong ones is often faster than finding the right one directly.

## Traps that cost points

- **Stopping at the first rule that fits two items.** Always check the full sequence.
- **Tracking only one feature.** Shapes can change in two ways at once, such as count and direction.
- **Reversing a logical statement.** "All red boxes are heavy" does not mean all heavy boxes are red.
- **Holding every condition in your head.** Jot them down or picture them in order.
- **Overthinking an easy item.** In an adaptive game, early items are often simple on purpose.
- **Losing heart after a hard round.** It may mean you have moved up a level. HireVue advises keeping going.

## Timing

HireVue says each game is under 20 minutes. As our guidance, aim to spot the rule within about 30 seconds on a practice item. If nothing appears, change what you are looking at: switch from rows to columns, or from shapes to counts.

## Practice items

These 13 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from HireVue or any game. Work without notes for items 1 to 6, and with scrap paper if you like for items 7 to 13.

**Item 1.** What comes next? **A, C, F, J, O, ?**

**Item 2.** What comes next? **Z, X, V, T, ?**

**Item 3.** What comes next? **square, triangle, circle, square, triangle, ?**

**Item 4.** An arrow points up, then right, then down. If the pattern continues, where does it point next?

**Item 5.** In a code, CAT is written as DBU. Using the same rule, how is DOG written?

**Item 6.** Which is the odd one out? **16, 25, 36, 48, 64**

**Item 7.** All red boxes are heavy. Box 4 is heavy. Must box 4 be red?

**Item 8.** Ana finished before Ben. Cal finished after Ben. Dee finished before Ana. Who finished first?

**Item 9.** In each row of a grid, the third number is the sum of the first two. One row reads **4, 9, ?**. What is the missing number?

**Item 10.** Row 1 of a pattern is: one circle, two circles, three circles. Row 2 is: one triangle, two triangles, ?. What goes in the gap?

**Item 11.** A key is in exactly one of three places: the drawer, the box or the shelf. Exactly one of these statements is true: (1) "The key is in the drawer." (2) "The key is not in the box." Where is the key?

**Item 12.** Four tasks, A, B, C and D, must be done one at a time. D must be first. B must come before A. C must come after A. In what order are they done?

**Item 13.** Look at this grid of numbers:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 4 | 8 |
| 3 | 6 | 12 |
| 5 | 10 | ? |

What is the missing number?

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 13</summary>

**Item 1: U.** As positions in the alphabet: A = 1, C = 3, F = 6, J = 10, O = 15. The gaps are 2, 3, 4, 5, so the next gap is 6. 15 + 6 = 21, which is U.

**Item 2: R.** Each letter goes back two places: Z, X, V, T, R.

**Item 3: circle.** The three shapes repeat in the same order.

**Item 4: left.** The arrow turns a quarter turn clockwise each time: up, right, down, left.

**Item 5: EPH.** Each letter moves one place forward: C to D, A to B, T to U. So D to E, O to P, G to H.

**Item 6: 48.** The others are square numbers: 4 × 4, 5 × 5, 6 × 6 and 8 × 8. 48 is not a square.

**Item 7: No.** The rule says red boxes are heavy. It says nothing about heavy boxes having to be red. A heavy blue box would fit every fact.

**Item 8: Dee.** Dee is before Ana, Ana is before Ben, and Cal is after Ben. The order is Dee, Ana, Ben, Cal.

**Item 9: 13.** 4 + 9 = 13.

**Item 10: three triangles.** Each row counts up from one to three, with a different shape in each row.

**Item 11: the shelf.** If the key were in the drawer, both statements would be true. If it were in the box, both would be false. Only on the shelf is exactly one statement true: (1) is false and (2) is true.

**Item 12: D, B, A, C.** D is fixed first. B must come before A, and C after A, so the remaining three go B, A, C.

**Item 13: 20.** Each row doubles from left to right: 5, 10, 20. Columns also work: the third column is 8, 12, 20, which matches four times the first column (2, 3, 5).

</details>

## Score yourself

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Answer | U | R | circle | left | EPH | 48 | No | Dee | 13 | three triangles | shelf | D, B, A, C | 20 |

- **12 to 13:** strong pattern and logic skills. Practise harder visual puzzles where two features change at once.
- **9 to 11:** check whether misses were sequences (items 1 to 6) or logic (7 to 13). For logic, write the conditions down every time.
- **8 or fewer:** go back to the method and do one item a day slowly, saying each step out loud, before working on speed.
