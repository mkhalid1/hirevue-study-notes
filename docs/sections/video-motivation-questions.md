# Motivation and strengths questions

**In one line:** you explain why you want this job at this employer and what you bring to it, using specific reasons and real evidence.

These questions are not one of HireVue's named question types. They are simply in most interviews, recorded or live: "Why do you want to work here?", "Why this role?", "Tell us about yourself", "What is your greatest strength?" HireVue's interview tips tell you to treat a HireVue interview like any other interview, starting with research into the job and the company. This page turns that research into answers.

## What it measures

Employers ask motivation questions to find out three things:

- **Do you understand the job?** People who know what the work involves day to day are more likely to stay and do well.
- **Is your interest real and specific?** Reasons that could apply to any company are a weak signal.
- **Can you back up what you say about yourself?** A strength with no example is just a claim.

## How the questions look

In our own words:

- "Tell us a little about yourself and why you applied."
- "Why do you want to work for us?"
- "What attracts you to this role?"
- "What would you say is your greatest strength? Give an example."
- "What is an area you are working to improve?"
- "Where would you like to be in a few years?"

## A method that works

For **"why us" and "why this role"**, use three parts. This is our structure.

1. **Something specific about them.** A product, a service, how they work, a value they act on, a part of the role's day-to-day work. Name it.
2. **Your link to it.** An experience, a skill or an interest that connects you to that thing, with a short example.
3. **What you would bring and what you want to learn.** One sentence each.

For **strengths**, use claim, example, relevance:

1. **Claim:** name one strength in plain words ("I stay organised when there is a lot going on").
2. **Example:** a short STAR-style story that proves it (see [past-behaviour questions](video-past-behaviour-questions.md)).
3. **Relevance:** why it matters in this role.

For **areas to improve**, use real, managed, improving: a real weakness that does not rule you out of the job, what you are doing about it, and evidence that it is getting better.

For **"tell us about yourself"**, use present, past, future in about a minute: what you do now, one or two things from your past that led here, and why this role is the next step.

Research to do before the interview, in about an hour:

- Read the job advert line by line and list the three or four tasks that come up most.
- Read the employer's careers pages and one recent news item or product update.
- Write one sentence for each: "What I like about them is...", "What links me to it is...", "What I'd bring is..."

## Traps that cost points

- **Reasons that fit any employer.** "You're a big, successful company" says nothing about this one.
- **Only what you get.** Pay, location and "it would look good on my CV" are real reasons, but on their own they do not show interest in the work.
- **Reading the website back.** Quoting the mission statement is not the same as saying why it matters to you.
- **Strengths without proof.** Always attach an example.
- **Fake weaknesses.** "I'm a perfectionist" and "I work too hard" are heard constantly and read as dodging.
- **A weakness at the heart of the job.** Saying you dislike talking to people, in a customer role, raises a real concern.
- **A life story.** "Tell us about yourself" is about the professional you, in about a minute.

## Timing

Your answer time is set by the employer. As our guidance, a "why us" or strengths answer works well at 60 to 90 seconds even when more time is allowed. A short, specific answer usually lands better than a long, general one. Use any spare time for one more piece of evidence, not repetition.

## Practice items

These 3 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from HireVue or any employer. Each has one best option.

**Item 1.** "Why do you want to work for us?" for a role at a regional bus company. Which answer is best?

- A. "You're a well-known company with a good reputation, and I think it would be a great place to work."
- B. "I've used your night buses for two years, and the new real-time app made a big difference to me. The role works on that app's customer feedback, and in my retail job I handled about 30 queries a day, so I'd like to bring that to a team that's improving a service I rely on."
- C. "The pay is good and the office is close to where I live."

**Item 2.** "What is your greatest strength?" Which answer is best?

- A. "I'm hard-working, reliable, organised, creative and a great team player."
- B. "My strength is that I'm a people person."
- C. "Staying organised under pressure. During exam season last year I ran the student union shop's stock ordering while sitting four exams. I planned orders a week ahead on one sheet and we didn't run out of anything. In this role, with several clients' deadlines at once, that planning habit would help."

**Item 3.** "What is an area you are working to improve?" Which answer is best?

- A. "Public speaking used to make me rush. I joined a weekly speaking club and now record myself presenting. At my last team update my manager said my pace was much better, and I'm still working on handling questions on the spot."
- B. "I'm a perfectionist, so I sometimes care too much about my work."
- C. "I don't really have any weaknesses that would affect my work."

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 3</summary>

**Item 1: B.** It names something specific about the employer (the app), links it to real experience, and says what you would bring. *Why not A:* it could be said about any well-known company. *Why not C:* pay and location are real reasons but show no interest in the work.

**Item 2: C.** One clear strength, a short example that proves it, and a link to the role. *Why not A:* a list of adjectives proves nothing. *Why not B:* a claim with no evidence.

**Item 3: A.** A real weakness that does not rule you out, a concrete plan and evidence of progress. *Why not B:* a disguised strength reads as avoiding the question. *Why not C:* everyone has something to work on, and saying otherwise sounds unaware.

</details>

## Worked examples

### Worked example 1: "Why do you want this role?" (about 180 words, 75 seconds spoken)

| Part | Notes |
|---|---|
| Specific to them | The role supports small businesses switching to the employer's booking system. |
| Your link | Helped my aunt's hair salon move from a paper diary to online bookings. Set it up, trained two staff, missed bookings dropped. |
| What you bring and want to learn | Patience explaining tech to busy people. Want to learn how onboarding works at scale. |

### Worked example 2: "What is your greatest strength?" (about 200 words)

| Part | Notes |
|---|---|
| Claim | I explain complicated things simply. |
| Example | Volunteer maths tutor for Year 9. Student failing fractions. Switched to pizza slices and money, weekly ten-minute quiz. Went from 4 out of 20 to 15 out of 20 over a term. |
| Relevance | The role answers customer questions about billing. Clear explanations save repeat calls. |

### Worked example 3: build your own

Write your three "why us" sentences for the job you are applying for. Then record a 75-second answer, watch it back, and check whether a friend could tell which company it was for without being told.

## Score yourself

| Item | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Answer | B | C | A |

- **3:** you know what a strong answer looks like. Now do the research for your actual employer.
- **2:** most misses here are about being general. Add one specific fact and one example to every answer.
- **1 or fewer:** reread the three-part method and redo the items after writing your own "why us" sentences.
