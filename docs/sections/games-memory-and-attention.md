# Memory and attention games

**In one line:** short tasks where you hold information in mind for a few seconds, follow changing rules, or spot a small difference, usually with the pace increasing as you go.

HireVue does not publish its game names or rules. Its articles say its cognitive games include tasks such as **memorising letters and numbers**, and its writing on game-based assessment describes memory recall games (remembering sequences or patterns after a brief look), attention and focus tasks (noticing subtle changes) and multitasking challenges (keeping track of more than one stream of information). These are the skills this page trains.

## What it measures

- **Working memory:** holding a few items in mind and doing something with them, such as recalling them in order or reversed.
- **Sustained attention:** staying accurate on a repetitive task when it would be easy to drift.
- **Selective attention:** noticing the one detail that matters and ignoring the rest.
- **Rule-following and switching:** applying a rule, then a different rule when the game tells you to.

Most people can hold about a handful of unrelated items in mind at once. Training does not change that much, but good strategies (grouping, saying items silently, using a rhythm) make a real difference to what you can recall.

## How the questions look

In our own words, the shapes HireVue describes look like this on screen:

- a sequence of letters, digits or lit squares appears for a few seconds, then disappears, and you reproduce it
- two items or lists appear and you say whether they match
- a rule is shown ("tap left for even numbers") and items flow past quickly
- the rule changes partway through

Games are randomized and adapt to your level, so sequences usually get longer or faster after correct answers.

## A method that works

1. **Read the instructions fully, every time.** Most mistakes in these games come from misreading the rule, not from poor memory.
2. **Group items.** Remember "K7R 2M9" as two chunks, not six separate characters. Phone numbers work the same way.
3. **Say it silently.** Repeat the sequence in your head in a steady rhythm while it is on screen.
4. **Use the space.** For positions on a grid, picture a path ("top-left, middle, bottom-right is a diagonal line").
5. **For matching, compare in chunks from left to right.** Check the first two characters, then the next two. Do not glance at the whole thing.
6. **When the rule changes, pause for half a second.** A tiny reset prevents a run of errors with the old rule.
7. **Let a miss go.** HireVue's own advice is to keep going after a failed round.

## Traps that cost points

- **Starting before you have read the rule.** Speed matters less than getting the rule right.
- **Trying to hold everything as single items.** Chunking is the biggest single improvement.
- **Rechecking an answer you already gave.** Your attention is then not on the next item.
- **Carrying the old rule after a switch.** The first two or three items after a switch are where errors cluster.
- **Playing tired or distracted.** These tasks are sensitive to fatigue. HireVue advises a distraction-free place and at least 20 minutes set aside.
- **Writing things down** when the game is meant to test memory. It breaks the rules of a fair assessment.

## Timing

HireVue says each game is under 20 minutes. These games set their own pace, so your job is to keep accuracy as the pace rises. For the drills below, look at each sequence for about 5 seconds, then cover it with your hand or a piece of paper before reading the question.

## Practice items

These 3 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from HireVue or any game. For items 1 and 2, look at the sequence or rule for about 5 seconds, cover it, then read the question. Item 3 tests attention: do it as quickly as you can while staying accurate.

**Item 1.** Sequence: **K 7 R 2 M 9**. Cover it. Say the sequence in reverse order.

**Item 2.** Rule: **press blue for an even number, red for an odd number, and press nothing for 0**. Cover the rule. What do you press for 7, then for 0, then for 12?

**Item 3.** List A: **3471, 9052, 6618, 2290**. List B: **3471, 9025, 6618, 2290**. How many pairs (first with first, second with second, and so on) are different?

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 3</summary>

**Item 1: 9 M 2 R 7 K.** Reverse each chunk and swap their order: "2M9" becomes "9M2", "K7R" becomes "R7K".

**Item 2: red, nothing, blue.** 7 is odd, 0 has its own rule, and 12 is even. The special case for 0 is the part people forget.

**Item 3: 1.** Only the second pair differs: 9052 against 9025. The other three pairs match.

</details>

## Score yourself

| Item | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Answer | 9M2R7K | red, nothing, blue | 1 |

- **3:** good recall and focus. Practise longer sequences (seven or eight items) to build headroom.
- **2:** check whether misses were memory (items 1 and 2) or attention (item 3). For memory, practise chunking. For attention, slow down slightly and compare in pairs.
- **1 or fewer:** do five minutes of digit-span practice a day: have someone read you sequences, or write them on cards, and recall them forwards and backwards.
