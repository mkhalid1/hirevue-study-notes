# How HireVue assesses you

Short version: HireVue does not publish a score scale, a pass mark or the list of games. What it does publish is how its assessments are built: each model is designed for a specific job, it looks at job-related competencies, it does not use facial recognition to score you, and people make the hiring decisions. This page separates what is published from what is not, so you can prepare for the right things.

## What HireVue says it assesses

From HireVue's candidate FAQ, in summary:

- **Job-specific models.** Each assessment model "is designed for a specific job role", and some are built for a particular company. There is no single assessment that all companies use.
- **Competencies, not trivia.** The assessment evaluates answers to questions about skills and competencies for that job. HireVue's examples are teamwork, persistence and, for a sales job, negotiation.
- **Whole answers.** HireVue says its assessments look at the whole context of a response, not just certain words.
- **What it does not look at.** HireVue says its assessments never look at age, race, gender, or résumé items such as GPA or the college you attended.
- **No facial recognition for scoring.** "Hirevue does not use facial recognition to score candidates." Some employers use separate technology to check your identity.

For games, HireVue's product page says they cover three domains (Personality and Work Style, Working with People, Working with Information), are randomized, and move you up or down levels based on your performance. It also says HireVue's psychologists work with each employer to choose the right mix of games.

## What is not published

| Question | Published? |
|---|---|
| A score scale (for example out of 100) | Not published |
| A pass mark | Not published. Each employer decides how to use the results. |
| The number of games | Not published. Set per employer. |
| The names and rules of each game | Not published |
| Think time, answer time and retakes for video questions | Set by the employer and shown in your setup screens |
| Whether you receive feedback | Depends on the employer. HireVue's articles mention that games can give candidates automated feedback. |

Be wary of sites that quote a "HireVue pass score" or a definitive list of games. HireVue itself does not publish either.

## How results are used

HireVue's FAQ says that for most jobs the interview, and the assessment if the employer uses one, help recruiters "make the first cut". People in the top group then move on to person-to-person interviews, and hiring decisions are made by people. In practice, think of the HireVue step as a screening interview: its job is to decide who the employer talks to next.

This has a useful consequence. Anything you say in a HireVue answer may come up again in a later live interview. Real stories and honest answers hold up. Polished inventions do not.

## What you can control

This part is our guidance, not HireVue's.

- **For video answers:** answer the question asked, with a specific example, in a clear order. The section guides on [past-behaviour](sections/video-past-behaviour-questions.md), [situational](sections/video-situational-questions.md) and [motivation](sections/video-motivation-questions.md) questions train exactly that.
- **For delivery:** be audible, well lit and steady. HireVue's tips say you don't need to worry about looking into the camera, and its FAQ says it does not use facial recognition to score you, so put your energy into what you say. See [setup and recording](setup-and-recording.md).
- **For games:** accuracy first, then speed. Keep going after a hard round, because adaptive games get harder as you do well.
- **For work-style items:** answer as you really are. A profile built from guesses about the employer is no help to you in a job you then have to do.

HireVue's FAQ says plainly that no course can help you "game" its assessments. Our view is the same. The preparation that works is the same preparation that works for any interview.

## Can you fail?

There is no published pass mark, so there is no fixed "fail" line. An employer may decide not to move you forward, which in practice feels the same. Most employers have their own rules on whether you can reapply. Check the role's application page or ask the recruiter.
